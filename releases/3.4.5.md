# AutoTrim Exporter 3.4.5

A correctness release on top of 3.4.4, driven by a field report and a full
engine audit. Two fixes matter to every delivery: a combined render could pick
up material you never selected, and every WAV delivery with a forced bit depth
shipped at the wrong depth. Alongside them: a filesystem-safety fix, honest QC
verdicts for peak targets, and three UCS naming fixes.
Rendering engine 2.11.6.

The interface is unchanged from 3.4.4 — see the
[3.4.3 interface tour](3.4.3.md) for the visual walkthrough.

## Fixed: a combined render could pick up the next sound on its own track

**If you selected one sound and another variation sat further along the same
track, the delivered file could contain both, separated by silence.**

Combined render mixes your selection through the master, so before rendering,
AutoTrim silences every unrelated item on the timeline. That pass deliberately
spares the tracks your selected audio actually travels through: the item's own
track, its parents, anything joined by a send, and the siblings of any folder
that path passes through. Without that exemption, a sidechain source, a MIDI
track driving a synth, or a parallel layer would go quiet and your sound would
render differently from how it plays.

The exemption was too broad. Those same tracks also carry the everyday
neighbours of a sound-design session — the next variation, ten seconds further
along the same track. And the analysis pass does not stop at the end of your
selection: it opens the render bounds past it to catch a reverb tail, by five
seconds, or by up to thirty when you allow three render passes. A spared
neighbour sitting inside that window stayed audible. The boundary detector saw
audio there, read it as a continuation of your sound, and extended the delivery
to cover it. The result was a file containing a sound, a silence, and a sound
you never selected — longer than your selection and wrong for the library.

An exempt item now stays audible only while it actually plays across the span of
your selection, which is the only window in which it can influence the selected
material at all. Everything else on those tracks is silenced for the render and
restored the moment it finishes, exactly like any other unrelated item.

**What is unaffected:** overlapping layers, sidechain sources and folder
siblings that play underneath your selection behave exactly as before — they are
still spared, because they still overlap. Per-item delivery never had this
defect at all: it renders through REAPER's own selected-items source, which does
not mix the rest of the timeline.

**If you were hit by this,** the symptom is a delivered file noticeably longer
than what you selected, containing extra hits with gaps between them. Re-export
those assets on 3.4.5; nothing else needs changing in your session.

## Fixed: forced WAV bit depths were encoded wrongly — every one of them

**If your delivery profile forces a WAV bit depth — and the Game/Wwise, Unreal,
EBU R128 and ATSC built-in profiles all do — the file did not ship at that
depth.**

REAPER's WAV render configuration stores the literal bit count in its depth
byte. The exporter wrote an enumerated index instead: for a 24-bit delivery it
sent the byte `2`, which REAPER reads as "2 bit cADPCM", and REAPER fell back
to whatever depth it chose. The delivery report never caught it, because the
delivered file's bit depth is only reported, never judged. The encoding was
verified byte-for-byte against an independent implementation
(Ultraschall's `CreateRenderCFG_WAV`), and the regression tests that had locked
in the wrong bytes were corrected together with the fix.

**Check your library:** if you delivered WAV with a forced bit depth, inspect a
few files' actual format and re-export what does not match the profile.
Auto-depth WAV deliveries (no forced depth) were always correct.

## Fixed: a directory could be mistaken for a delivery file

On Linux and macOS, the check deciding "this path is a regular file" also
accepted directories. In Replace mode, a directory named like the delivery —
say an unpacked `Impact.wav` folder sitting in the export directory — would be
renamed aside as a delivery backup, moving the whole directory. The check now
requires an actual read, the one operation a directory refuses. Windows was
never affected.

## Honest QC: peak targets are judged, not just printed

The delivery report printed your normalization target in every mode but only
judged it for LUFS-I. A Peak-normalized delivery landing at −6 dBFS against a
−1 dBFS target printed the target next to a clean PASS. Peak and True Peak
targets are now verified — with your declared tolerance, or ±0.5 dB where none
is declared, since fades and lossy codecs legitimately move a peak by fractions
of a dB. An RMS target now says plainly that the delivery RMS is never
measured, instead of implying a verified PASS.

Deliberately unchanged: with level processing set to *Keep original*, no
limiter is applied and no ceiling is enforced. Keep original means keep
original; the game-audio profiles ship dry by design.

## Fixed: UCS names can no longer mean the wrong fields

A UCS filename is positional — every field's meaning depends on its place.
Three ways a field could silently change places, all closed:

- **CreatorID `Acme/Sound` no longer becomes two fields.** Forbidden filename
  characters were replaced with `_` — the field separator itself — *after* the
  fields were validated. They now become `-` inside the field, before the name
  is assembled.
- **A custom CatID with underscores is rewritten** (`MY_CUSTOM_ID` →
  `MY-CUSTOM-ID`) and the warning shows the exact spelling that was written.
- **Empty required fields keep their position.** An empty CreatorID or SourceID
  used to be dropped, shifting every later field one place left; it is now
  filled with `NONE`, and the warning says so.

If your pipeline parses UCS names, files exported with affected field values on
older versions may carry misaligned fields — worth a spot check.

## Upgrading

Replace the installed scripts with the ones in this package. Your settings,
delivery profiles, name lists and export history are stored by REAPER and carry
over untouched.
